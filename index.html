Complete Laboratory Manual & Theoretical Background

Standardized Class 10 Biology Practical Protocol • DIGITALCLASSES DIGILAB

1.1 Aim of the Experiment

  • A. To prepare a stained temporary mount of an onion epidermal peel and observe key eukaryotic plant cell organelles (cell wall, plasma membrane, cytoplasm, large central vacuole, and peripheral nucleus).
  • B. To prepare a squash mount of onion root tip meristematic cells and identify the distinct stages of somatic cell division (Mitosis): Interphase, Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, and Telophase.
Duration: 45 Minutes • Biosafety Level: BSL-1 (Standard School Lab)

1.2 Apparatus, Biological Specimen & Reagents

Biological Material & Chemicals
  • • Fresh healthy Onion bulb (Allium cepa) with actively growing roots (2–3 cm long)
  • • 2% Acetocarmine stain (binds specifically to chromatin/chromosomes)
  • • Safranin solution (for staining cellulose cell walls in epidermal peel)
  • • 1N Hydrochloric Acid (1N HCl) (hydrolyzes middle lamella pectin for easy squashing)
  • • 50% Glycerin (mounting medium to prevent desiccation) & Distilled water
Glassware & Laboratory Instruments
  • • Compound Light Microscope (10x eyepiece; 10x & 40x objective lenses)
  • • Clean optically flat Glass Microscope Slides & thin Coverslips
  • • Watch Glasses (2), Beaker with water, and Spirit Lamp / Hot plate
  • • Fine-tipped Forceps, Dissecting Needle, Camel-hair Brush, and Sharp Blade
  • • Filter / Blotting Paper & Dropper bottles

1.3 Theoretical Background

Why Onion (Allium cepa) is an Ideal Model Specimen: The translucent inner epidermis of onion bulb scales consists of a single uniform layer of large, rectangular, brick-like cells devoid of chloroplasts, allowing crystal-clear visualization of the rigid cellulose cell wall, peripheral nucleus, and large central vacuole. Simultaneously, the apical meristematic zone (located 1–2 mm behind the root cap) of sprouting onion roots undergoes rapid, continuous mitotic cell division, and its 16 large chromosomes stain intensely with acetocarmine.

1. Interphase (Preparatory) G1 • S • G2

Metabolically active non-dividing phase (~85–90% of cell cycle). DNA replication occurs during the S-phase. Chromosomes exist as a diffuse, uncoiled chromatin network enclosed within an intact nuclear membrane with 1–2 prominent nucleoli.

2. Prophase (Condensation) M-Phase I

Chromatin fibers coil and condense into distinct thread-like chromosomes, each composed of two sister chromatids joined at a centromere. By late prophase, the nuclear envelope and nucleolus disintegrate, and mitotic spindle microtubules begin assembling.

3. Metaphase (Alignment) M-Phase II

Chromosomes reach maximum condensation and attach via kinetochores to spindle fibers. All chromosomes align precisely along the cell's central equatorial plane, forming the characteristic Metaphase Plate—the ideal stage for karyotyping.

4. Anaphase (Segregation) M-Phase III

Centromeres split simultaneously. Separated sister chromatids (now daughter chromosomes) are pulled toward opposite poles by shortening kinetochore microtubules, assuming characteristic V, J, L, or I shapes depending on centromere position.

5. Telophase (Reconstitution) M-Phase IV

Daughter chromosomes arrive at opposite poles, uncoil back into chromatin, and two new nuclear membranes and nucleoli reassemble around each polar cluster. Spindle fibers depolymerize.

6. Cytokinesis (Cell Plate) Division End

Unlike animal cells that form a cleavage furrow, rigid plant cells divide their cytoplasm centrifugally via Golgi-derived vesicles fusing at the equator to form a Phragmoplast & Cell Plate, maturing into a new middle lamella and cell wall.

1.4 Step-by-Step Laboratory Procedure

  1. 1 Root Germination: Place a clean onion bulb over a beaker filled with water so only its basal disc touches the water surface. Incubate at room temperature for 3–4 days until white adventitious roots grow 2–3 cm long.
  2. 2 Harvesting Meristematic Tips: Cut 2–3 mm terminal root tips using a sharp blade during morning hours (peak mitotic activity) and transfer them into a watch glass containing 1N HCl.
  3. 3 Acid Hydrolysis & Washing: Gently warm the watch glass over a flame for 1–2 minutes (do not boil) to soften the intercellular pectic middle lamella. Transfer tips to distilled water to rinse off excess acid.
  4. 4 Acetocarmine Staining: Transfer the hydrolyzed root tips into a clean watch glass containing 3–4 drops of 2% Acetocarmine and warm gently for 3–5 minutes until the apical tips turn deep crimson-red.
  5. 5 Squash Mount Preparation: Place one stained root tip on a clean glass slide in a drop of 50% glycerin. Lower a coverslip at a 45° angle using a needle to prevent air bubbles. Place blotting paper over the slide and press gently with your thumb to spread the cells into a single monolayer.
  6. 6 Microscopic Examination: Mount the slide on the compound microscope stage. Locate the dividing zone under low power (10x objective = 100x total), then switch to high power (40x objective = 400x total) to identify and count cells in each mitotic phase.

1.5 Essential Precautions

  • ✓ Always harvest root tips in the morning (8:00 AM – 11:00 AM) when mitotic index is highest.
  • ✓ Do not overheat or boil the stain/acid, as excessive heat chars and destroys delicate chromosomes.
  • ✓ Lower the coverslip gently at a 45° angle with a mounting needle to avoid trapping air bubbles.
  • ✓ Apply vertical thumb pressure without lateral sliding so chromosomes are not sheared or scattered.
  • ✓ Focus initially with the coarse adjustment under 10x, then use only the fine adjustment knob under 40x.
Safety Note: Handle 1N HCl and Acetocarmine with droppers; wipe any excess stain around the coverslip with filter paper before placing on the microscope stage.

Interphase Intact double nuclear membrane; 1–2 prominent nucleoli visible. Uncoiled, diffuse chromatin reticulum; no individual chromosomes seen. Large uniformly stained spherical nucleus occupying central cytoplasm.
